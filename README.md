# Arespectfulness

Invisible Inheritance and the Stack of Solved Impossibilities.

Modern agents act through inheritances they did not build and seldom perceive: an oxygenated atmosphere produced by cyanobacteria, the mitochondrial machinery that supplies neural energy, controlled fire, written memory, and the compilers that turn source code into machine behavior. We name the resulting failure of perception arespect: the absence of felt regard for the depth, cost, and difficulty of an enabling condition, which settles once a hard-won achievement stabilizes into a reliable interface. Arespect differs from disrespect and from ingratitude, both of which presuppose that the thing has been perceived. Its mechanism, stack blindness, is the withdrawal of a working layer from attention while the agent acts through it; the layer returns to view when it fails. A comparison with disenchantment (Weber), reification (Lukács), alienation, inattentional and change blindness, the withdrawal of the ready-to-hand (Heidegger), infrastructural invisibility (Star), black-boxing (Latour), and tacit knowing (Polanyi) shows that each describes the mechanism or the content of withdrawal, and none describes the dispositional failure of regard that withdrawal permits. The stack is then traced across scales, from the Great Oxidation Event around 2.4 to 2.3 billion years ago and the endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria, through the brain's consumption of about 20% of resting energy, to fire, writing, and software abstraction, with each empirical claim graded as established, plausible inference, or live controversy. The structural claim, that inherited difficulty drops from perception because of the success of what drops, rests only on the established layers. The proposed remedy is depth perception, including an accurate estimate of how much of the depth remains unknown.
