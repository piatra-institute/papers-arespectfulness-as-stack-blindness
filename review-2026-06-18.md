Both bibliographic errors confirmed, and the Poulton one is serious: the cited work is actually **Ostrander et al. 2024, *Nature* 631(8020):335–339** — Poulton is the *fifth* author, and the paper's bibliography invents a co-author list (Cumming, Zerkle, Mänd, Kunzmann, Wang, Goldberg, Reinhard, Canfield) that does not match the real paper. I have enough to write the report.

# Review: arespectfulness-as-stack-blindness

## Verdict
A fair but tough referee would say **accept-with-major-revisions**. The prose is genuinely alive, the cross-disciplinary command is real, the §8 self-grading table is an unusual and admirable instance of a paper enacting its own thesis, and 68 of 69 references survive verification cleanly. But the contribution is conceptual-synthetic, not empirical, and at its conceptual core the paper equivocates between two incompatible definitions of "arespect" (a *perceptual* failure vs. a *valuation* failure) and uses whichever one wins each contrast. The single most serious concrete problem: a load-bearing geological citation ("Poulton et al., 2024") is **confabulated** — wrong first author, a largely invented co-author list, wrong volume and pages — which is precisely the failure mode a paper this proud of its epistemic restraint cannot afford in its own apparatus.

## Claim grounding
- **"reading marks that took 4,000 years to standardize"** (Abstract; echoed in §6) → **unsupported as a number.** Schmandt-Besserat is cited for the token-origin thesis but nowhere supports a specific "4,000 years to standardize" duration. It's a rhetorical figure wearing a precise costume. Soften or source.
- **"risen permanently around 2.4 to 2.3 billion years ago"** (§4, §8) → **supported** (Lyons; Bekker; Ostrander-misfiled-as-Poulton). The hedging about a contested onset window is faithful to the sources.
- **"the capacity to make oxygen seems to predate its lasting accumulation by hundreds of millions of years"** (§4) → **supported** (Lyons et al. 2014).
- **"There is no direct evidence for a discrete mass extinction of the anaerobes"** (§4) → **supported and commendably restrained** (Sessions et al. 2009). This is the paper at its best — refusing the popular "oxygen holocaust."
- **"LUCA was anaerobic … bedrock"** (§4) → **supported.** The paper correctly quarantines the contested vent-metabolism inference (Weiss 2016) from the secure anaeroby claim.
- **"orders of magnitude more energy per gene … cleared the way for … complexity"** attributed to Lane & Martin, then **paired with its rebuttal** (Lynch & Marinov; Booth & Doolittle) (§5, §8) → **supported and correctly flagged as live controversy.** Verified that Lynch & Marinov is indeed a direct rebuttal.
- **"brain … about 2% of body mass and burns on the order of 20% … holds remarkably steady"** (§5) → **supported** (Raichle & Gusnard 2002, near-verbatim).
- **"the claim that the fold of a crista or the flux of a proton produces or explains conscious experience … is not licensed"** (§5) → **supported by the paper's own restraint and by Chen & Zhang.** This is the paper's strongest grounding move.
- **"habitual fire … only turns continuous much later, perhaps 400,000 to 300,000 years ago, which leaves the cooking hypothesis straining against its own clock"** (§6) → **supported** (Roebroeks & Villa 2011; Navarrete 2011). Honest about the contested joint.
- **"maintenance consumes far more of the world's technical effort than invention does"** (§9) → **supported** (Edgerton 2007; Russell & Vinsel).
- **"Arespect toward the stack runs continuous … with arespect toward the people who hold it up"** (§9) → **overstated / asserted.** The continuity between a *perceptual-evaluative disposition toward artifacts* and *political invisibility of maintenance labor* is declared, not argued; the Russell-Vinsel material is real but it is about institutional/economic neglect, not about the perceptual "arespect" the paper defines. The bridge between the two is the paper's, and it is rhetorical.

## Argument
- **§2 — The core distinction equivocates between perception and valuation.** To separate arespect from *disrespect*, the paper says arespect "comes before perception … nothing has registered as being there to weigh" — a **perceptual** failure. To separate it from *change blindness*, the §2 table says arespect is "intact perception with absent regard … a **valuation** failure." It cannot be both "perception is absent" and "perception is intact, only regard is absent." A charitable reconstruction (perception of the *object* is intact; perception of the object *as achievement/depth* is absent, which then forecloses valuation) is available and would fix this — but the paper never states it cleanly and instead deploys whichever horn wins the contrast in front of it. This is the deepest rigor problem, and it sits at the exact hinge the whole paper turns on.
- **§2 — The nearest neighbors are missing, not differentiated.** The differentiation exercise covers phenomenology (Heidegger, Polanyi) and STS (Star, Latour) but omits the literature most directly about *the taken-for-granted*: Marx's **commodity fetishism** (Capital, 1867 — the consumer not perceiving the social labor congealed in the commodity is far closer to "arespect toward sedimented labor" than the 1844 *alienation* the paper does cite), Schutz's **world-taken-for-granted**, Garfinkel's **"seen but unnoticed" background expectancies**, and Bourdieu's **doxa**. For a paper whose claim to novelty *is* the differentiation, these absences are not neutral — a referee in this space will read them as the nearest competitors going unmet.
- **§§4–5, 8 — "Difficulty" is anthropomorphized across the non-agentive layers.** The unifying figure ("a hard thing was solved and buried") is literal only where there is a solver and a problem — fire, writing, the compiler. Cyanobacterial oxygenation was not an "achievement," a "solved impossibility," or something "paid for"; it was a contingent geochemical transition with no agent and (for anaerobes) casualties. The title's "Stack of Solved Impossibilities" and §9's "accumulated impossibility" treat as identity what is, for geology/biology, analogy. The paper half-sees this ("the mitochondrion was designed by nothing at all," §7) but does not reckon with how much it costs the thesis that "the shape is the same whether the inherited layer is geological, biological, or technical" (§1). The shape is *similar*; the claim of sameness is carried by prose, not argument.
- **§8 — The empirical apparatus is conceded to be non-load-bearing, which makes it illustration, not evidence.** "A reader unconvinced that mitochondria caused complex life can throw that claim out and lose nothing here" (§8). True — and it reveals that the structural thesis is essentially *a priori* and unfalsifiable (any reliable inherited layer is, by definition, "looked through"). There is no observation that could count against "arespect." That is fine for a conceptual paper, but the paper presents itself in an empirical register (dates, energy budgets, a graded evidence table) that implies the biology is doing evidential work it explicitly disclaims. The honest framing — *this is a concept paper; the science is vivid illustration of a definitional claim* — is never stated.
- **§2 vs §9 — Performative tension in the cure.** If "arespect comes before perception, and argument has nothing to grip" (§2), then a paper, which is nothing but argument, cannot dislodge it. The remedy in §9 ("look again") is an exhortation of exactly the kind §2 says has nothing to grip. The paper needs, and never gives, an account of why *its* description restores perception where argument cannot.
- **Normativity is asserted.** The paper calls arespect a "vice," a "pathology," a "failure," while also insisting the arespectful person can be "entirely innocent." Why one *ought* to perceive inherited difficulty — what is lost, to whom, beyond the borrowed maintainer argument — is never grounded. The "ought" is slid in from a phenomenological "is."

**What is genuinely strong:** the §8 self-grading table is a real methodological contribution and disarms the obvious "the biology is doing rhetorical work it can't support" charge by naming it first. The discipline around consciousness (refusing the crista-does-the-thinking move, flagging the metabolic-vs-variational "free energy" category error) is exactly right and rare. The reading of Plato's Thamus as "an instance of the thing this paper is about, a flash of vivid perception of a layer in the moment it is installed" is a genuine insight, not decoration.

## Factual errors
- **Citation, §4/§8:** "Poulton et al. (2024), *Nature* 630(8015):145–151" — wrong first author (Ostrander), wrong volume/issue/pages (631(8020):335–339), invented co-author list. Verified. (The *scientific* claim it supports is correct.)
- **Citation, §5:** Spang et al. (2015) title wrong ("…eukaryotic and prokaryotic divide" → "…gap between prokaryotes and eukaryotes"). Verified.
- **Reference/concept, §3/§9:** "tertiary retention" pinned to Stiegler vol. 1; the term is properly later (vol. 2). Verified at medium confidence.
- **Unsourced figure, Abstract/§6:** "4,000 years to standardize" has no source.
- No errors found in the dates (Wonderwerk ~1 Ma, GBY ~790 ka, "24 centuries" since Phaedrus, GOE window, Archean pO₂ < 10⁻⁵ PAL, brain 2%/20%) — all check out.

## Prose and insight
**Alive:** the opening ("breathing oxygen made by cyanobacteria … and feels none of it"); "a standing miracle as furniture"; "The king's forgetting arrived exactly on time"; "the compiler was designed to hide what it does, and the mitochondrion was designed by nothing at all"; "Information hiding is stack blindness written into a specification; the inability to see in is the feature on the order sheet." The a-privative coinage with the *respicere* ("to look again") etymology is elegant and earns its keep better than most neologisms.

**Dead / over-worked:** the central figure ("looked through and not at," "the floor was a mountain," "paid once and folded out of sight") is restated so many times that by §9 it has gone from argument to incantation; §9 in particular largely re-says §1 in the same cadence. "Everything that wanted to close it: damp, wind, exhaustion" and similar tricolons tip into purple. The repeated "Watch how short the step is" / "Hold onto that last clause" / "The line is right there" tutorial asides are mannered.

**The genuine contribution:** a disciplined *synthesis* — pulling Heidegger, Star, Latour, Polanyi, Simondon, Stiegler into one frame and extending Latour's "opacity manufactured by success" from a property of systems to a *disposition of the perceiver* — plus the enacted-restraint method of §8. That is real and publishable. What it is *not* is an empirical discovery or a demonstrated cross-scale invariance; it is a well-made concept and a name. The paper would be stronger if it admitted that plainly.

## Top fixes
1. **Fix the Poulton/Ostrander reference** (§4, §8, References). Correct to Ostrander, C. M., Heard, A. W., Shu, Y., Bekker, A., Poulton, S. W., Olesen, K. P., & Nielsen, S. G. (2024), *Nature* 631(8020):335–339, and change every in-text "Poulton et al. (2024)" to "Ostrander et al. (2024)." This is mandatory: an invented author list in a paper that stakes its credibility on epistemic discipline is the kind of thing a referee uses to doubt the other 68 references (which, for the record, are clean).
2. **Resolve the perceptual-vs-valuation equivocation in §2.** State once, explicitly, the two-aspect view (object perceived; object-as-achievement not perceived; valuation thereby foreclosed) and hold to it. Stop using "comes before perception" against disrespect and "intact perception, absent regard" against change blindness — pick the reconstruction that makes both contrasts on one definition.
3. **Engage the actual nearest neighbors.** Add and differentiate against Marx's **commodity fetishism** (not just 1844 alienation), Schutz's **taken-for-granted world**, Garfinkel's **"seen but unnoticed" background expectancies**, and Bourdieu's **doxa**. These are the strongest competitors to the claim that "no term for this exists"; meeting them will either sharpen the novelty or honestly bound it.
4. **Own the anthropomorphism of "difficulty."** Add a paragraph (the §7 "designed by nothing" line is the seed) conceding that "solved impossibility" is literal for the human/technical layers and analogical for the geological/biological ones, and argue *why the analogy still holds* rather than asserting sameness. This protects the title from the charge that it imports teleology into the GOE.
5. **State the genre honestly in §8.** One sentence conceding that the structural thesis is conceptual and not falsified-or-confirmed by the biology — that the science is illustration of a definitional claim — would convert the paper's biggest vulnerability (empirical dress on an a priori frame) into a declared scope. The §8 table already does this work; let the prose say it.
6. **Ground the normative claim.** Give one explicit argument for why failing to perceive inherited difficulty is a *defect* and not merely a neutral feature of bounded attention — what is lost, and to whom — so "vice/pathology" stops being asserted alongside "entirely innocent."
7. **Correct Spang's title and the Stiegler "tertiary retention" attribution; source or soften "4,000 years to standardize."** Low effort, removes three easy referee hits.
8. **Cut one full pass of the central metaphor** (especially in §9, which largely reprises §1). The figure is strong enough that restraint will make it land harder; repetition is currently spending its own capital.
